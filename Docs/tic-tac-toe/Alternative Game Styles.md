## Lock-In Style
> *Origin: this was "Option B" of the sending-rule question. Option A (Ultimate Tic Tac
> Toe style) was chosen as the real rule. Saved here for future reference.*

**The mechanic:** both players keep playing in the **same quadrant** until that small
board resolves — either someone wins it, or it fills up as a cat game. Only then does
play move to a new quadrant.

**How it differs from the chosen rule:** in the real game, every single move sends your
opponent somewhere new, so play bounces around the whole board constantly. In lock-in
style, play stays put and resolves one small board at a time before moving on.

**Open threads if we ever build this:**
- Who picks the next quadrant once the current one resolves — the winner, the loser, or
  the player whose turn it is?
- What if the picker chooses an already-claimed or cat-gamed quadrant?
- Does the first player still pick the opening quadrant?

**Why it might be worth revisiting:** it's a much gentler mechanic. Players only think
about one small board at a time instead of tracking where each move sends the opponent.
That could make it a good **easy mode or kids mode**, given kids are a target audience.
